# Pediatric Red Flags

This file is a conservative escalation checklist. It is not exhaustive.

Seek urgent professional care or emergency help for signals such as:

- trouble breathing, blue lips, severe wheezing, pauses in breathing, or
  struggling to speak or cry
- unresponsiveness, extreme lethargy, confusion, stiff neck, seizure, or
  persistent inconsolable crying
- signs of dehydration: no urine for many hours, very dry mouth, no tears when
  crying, sunken eyes, or unusual sleepiness
- fever in a very young infant, or any fever with stiff neck, purple rash,
  severe headache, breathing difficulty, or poor responsiveness
- severe allergic reaction: swelling of lips/tongue/face, breathing trouble, or
  widespread hives with vomiting or faintness
- poisoning, medication overdose, button battery, magnet ingestion, or exposure
  to dangerous chemicals
- serious injury, head injury with concerning symptoms, deep burns, heavy
  bleeding, or suspected fracture
- severe abdominal pain, testicular pain, persistent vomiting, bloody stool, or
  green vomit
- caregiver instinct that the child is seriously unwell

When in doubt, escalate rather than normalize. Local emergency numbers,
pediatric urgent care, and poison control are outside this repository and must
be chosen by the owner according to location.
